# I Tried to Replace Claude With Free AI Models

<PostDate date={date} />

I use AI every day, and I pay for it. So at some point the question was
obvious: can free models running on my own laptop do the same job? I spent
the last month finding out. Short answer: they do a lot of real work in my
projects, but they didn't replace **Claude**. The long answer is below.

## The machine

Everything here runs on one laptop: an i5-13420H, 62 GB of RAM and an RTX
3050 with 6 GB of VRAM. That last number decides almost everything. With
that much RAM a big model loads fine. With 6 GB of VRAM it just doesn't run
fast.

## The free models I actually use

These are the open models that ended up in my projects. All free, all
running on my own hardware.

- **qwen3:1.7b**, **llama3.2:3b** and **gemma3:4b**, in Ollama. The small
  trio behind Mini AI, for chat, shell commands and vision.
- **qwen3:8b** and **nomic-embed-text**, in Ollama. The brain of Digivice,
  my Digimon tamagotchi. The Digimon talks in character, remembers things
  about me and pulls lore from an index of 146 Wikimon pages built with the
  embedding model. About 10 seconds per reply.
- **qwen3-vl:4b**, in Ollama. It describes every page of a manga volume as
  JSON, so later I can search for things like "pages with a computer".
  Around 5 seconds per page on the GPU. Sometimes it invents an object, so
  the search looks at the top hits before trusting them. **gemma3:4b** did
  the same job, but it kept seeing swords and scrolls that weren't there.
- **qwen3 30B-A3B**, in Ollama and llama.cpp. The biggest one I run, used as
  the brain of Goose, a local agent. More on that below.
- **FLUX.2 klein 4B** and **Qwen-Image-Edit-2511**, in ComfyUI. Image
  editing. They run the generative fill and object removal in my GIMP, the
  text cleanup in my comic tools, and photo-restore, which repaired 141 old
  family prints. This is the part that worked best, so it has its own
  section below.
- **LaMa** and **rembg**. The old reliable ones, for inpainting flat
  screentone and cutting out backgrounds.

That's a lot of free AI doing real work. None of it replaced **Claude**.

## The plan: Mini AI

[Mini AI](https://github.com/diegochagas/mini-ai) started on August 29 as a
one-script install of a local AI stack. Ollama for the models, SearXNG for
web search, mcpo to expose MCP tools (filesystem, email, browser) and Open
WebUI as the chat. The first model set was **llama3.1:8b**, **qwen3:8b**
and **gemma3:12b**. The idea was simple: a private assistant that searches
the web, reads my PDFs, organizes files and checks my inbox, with no cloud
and no subscription.

I kept a TEST-LOG.md in the repo from day one and every real test went
there. That log is pretty much the story of why it didn't work out.

## Test 1: renaming 9 files lost 2 of them

On August 30 I asked **llama3.1:8b**, in Open WebUI with the filesystem
tool, to rename 9 images in reverse order. The folder went from 9 files to
7, then back to 9, but with different content. Two of the original images
were gone for good, I never found them anywhere on disk. My best guess is
that the model "renamed" them by reading the images as text and writing
them back under names it half invented.

Then I tried again, watching it this time, with one message listing all 9
exact source and destination pairs.

- First try, it printed the 9 calls as JSON and never called the tool.
- Second try, it called the tool 9 times and said everything worked. Zero
  files changed. It had swapped the real filenames for `file1.txt`,
  `file2.txt` and so on.
- Third try, one call per message. 7 of 9 worked. The last 2 failed because
  Open WebUI's editor turns a typed `1x1` into `1×1`, so the path never
  matched. Pasting instead of typing fixed it.

Three attempts, a browser console trick and 2 lost images, to rename 9
files.

## Going smaller, and writing the brain myself

The next day I went the other way. I switched to the small trio,
**qwen3:1.7b**, **llama3.2:3b** and **gemma3:4b**, about 6.7 GB in total,
small enough to run even on my 8 GB ZimaOS server. I also dropped Open
WebUI and wrote `terminal-ai.py`, a terminal client that sends each message
to chat, web search, vision, file reading, email or shell commands.

The full re-test looked good on paper. Chat, web search, PDF summary,
Japanese translation, OCR, CSV totals, email and single renames all passed.
But almost every pass came after a fix in the script, not in the model.

- The CSV total came back 329.40 instead of 330.40, until I added a
  "calculate step by step" instruction.
- The search for the latest F1 race had no date in it, until I put today's
  date in the router prompt.
- **gemma3:4b**'s own call on "does this image need a web search" was wrong
  in both directions, so now the script decides with keywords.
- The Japanese and Chinese OCR came back partly garbled. **gemma3:4b** just
  can't match **gemma3:12b** there.
- The 9-file reverse rename still failed. Neither small model wrote a safe
  command. Nothing broke only because the script asks y/N before running
  anything.

So I rebuilt batch renames with the model doing the minimum. The script
lists the real files, the model only returns an old→new JSON mapping, the
script checks it (no invented files, no collisions, no overwrites) and
renames one file at a time through a staging folder. That one passed, byte
for byte.

Web research went the same way. I gave it a Mercado Livre product JSON with
6 empty fields. The first run filled 0 of 6, because the model put every
search in quotes and ignored the ISBN. After the script started stripping
the quotes and forcing an ISBN search, it filled 4 of 6 correctly and said
"not found" for the other 2 instead of making something up.

Email too. I asked how many unread emails I had in my inbox and it said
246, which was the total of every folder, with INBOX: 3 right there in the
data. One more line in the prompt fixed it.

Later I added a fallback to web search when the chat model doesn't know the
answer, and a build mode that creates a small project. The build test made
a working calculator web app with the four operations in 1m23s on CPU. But
the model wrapped every file in markdown fences even after being told not
to, and the README it wrote was generic boilerplate.

The lesson went into the README: small models can't be trusted with rules.
Anything that has to hold goes into script code, and the model only handles
language. That works. But at that point I wasn't building an assistant
anymore, I was hand-coding every path an assistant would take. **Claude**
doesn't need me to write a Python guard every time it does something.

## Going bigger: Goose and a 30B model

If small models were the problem, the next step was a bigger one. On
September 19 I set up Goose, an open-source agent, as a free alternative to
**Claude**'s Cowork, with Ollama as the provider.

The test task was simple: fix a Python script, sum a CSV (the answer is
77.4), move some files and write a summary.

- **qwen3:8b** with Ollama's default 4k context failed. Goose's own prompt
  is about 7k tokens, so the model was working half blind. It never retried
  a failed edit and wrote a summary with a literal `[total]` in it.
- **qwen3 30B-A3B**, the thinking version, with 16k context: 4 of 4, in
  about 12 minutes. Most of that was thinking.
- **qwen3 30B-A3B Instruct** with 16k context: 4 of 4, in about 5 minutes,
  and it recovered from its own errors. It used 14k of the 16k, so I moved
  the default to 32k.

That's where my laptop became the problem. The model is 18 GB and there
are 6 GB of VRAM. Ollama splits it between GPU and RAM, and I get about 21
tokens per second. I tried llama.cpp with MoE offload flags to squeeze out
more. Prompt processing went from about 270 to about 596 tokens per second,
but generation stayed at about 20, because it's limited by RAM bandwidth.
No flag fixes that.

Then the real test, a browser task. Goose looped for about an hour asking
for the page's DOM, got the whole 242k-character GitHub page back, grepped
it for nothing useful and never tried the simpler JavaScript tool. With
the 32k context full, speed fell to 0.3 tokens per second.

Something **Claude** does in a minute took an hour and didn't even finish.

## Qwen and FLUX in ComfyUI, for my comics and for GIMP

Image editing is where free models really paid off. The same 6 GB GPU that
chokes on a 30B chat model handles two image models fine, as long as they
are the right versions.

- **Qwen-Image-Edit-2511** is 20B, way too big for 6 GB. What makes it run
  is a 4-bit GGUF of it (through the ComfyUI-GGUF node) plus the 4-step
  Lightning LoRA. It still spills into RAM, about 23 GB of it, and takes
  around 100 to 115 seconds per edit. Better detail and better at following
  instructions.
- **FLUX.2 klein 4B** in fp8 is small and already distilled to 4 steps.
  About 30 to 35 seconds per edit. Three times faster than **Qwen**, a bit
  weaker on detailed art, but its fills look more natural.

Both are Apache 2.0, both keep the parts of the image you didn't ask to
change.

The install lives in [gimp-setup](https://github.com/diegochagas/gimp-setup),
as one more file in `features/`. It's opt-in: set `COMFYUI_DIR` in
`config.sh` and pick the model sets with `COMFYUI_MODEL_SETS` (`qwen` is
about 22 GB, `klein` about 12 GB). The script installs ComfyUI in its own
venv, adds the GGUF node, downloads every model from Hugging Face and
checks each file against its SHA-256. ComfyUI becomes a user service that
doesn't start at boot, because it holds the GPU. I start it when I need it:

```bash
systemctl --user start comfyui
```

### Comic text cleanup

My comic tools erase the text from manga pages so I can letter them in
Portuguese. Before, that meant a paid model or **LaMa**. The first local
test, on a Saint Seiya page in Chinese, was bad: giving the whole page to
**Qwen** turned spiky balloons into ovals, missed text over the art and
shifted everything a few pixels.

What works is not letting the model decide what to touch. A text detector
finds the text blocks. Blocks on a plain background just get a solid fill.
Only blocks over art get model pixels, after the model's output is aligned
back to the original. Everything else stays the original page.

I tested it on 17 text blocks over art from 6 pages. **FLUX.2 klein** was
the best at redrawing structure, hair, eyes, armor, but it missed one block
and left grid artifacts on two screentone areas. **Qwen** never missed, but
it flattens screentone into smooth gray and once invented an ear. **LaMa**
was the best on flat screentone and smeared everything else. So the script
measures the area around each block: flat halftone goes to **LaMa**, the
rest goes to **Qwen**, and if **Qwen** misses a block it falls back to
**LaMa**. In comic-skills it's one flag:

```bash
python clean-texts/scripts/clean_texts.py "<image-or-folder>" --backend local
```

### GIMP AI tools

The AI plug-ins in my GIMP (Generative Fill, Image Generator and Remove
Selection) used to call OpenAI or Gemini. I don't have access to those, so
now they only talk to ComfyUI on localhost, with **FLUX.2 klein** or
**Qwen** as the choice. Flatpak GIMP shares the host network, so it just
works, as long as ComfyUI is already running.

The hard part was not the connection, it was what the model gets to see of
the hole. I measured it on both models:

- A gray hole: fine on small holes in photos, but when the hole was half the
  crop the model returned it untouched.
- The original still visible, with a "remove" prompt: excellent for text
  over halftone and paper texture, but **FLUX.2 klein** redrew the object
  instead of removing it.
- The hole hidden under a blurry pre-fill, with "restore the blurry patch":
  the best for removing objects, but it left a flat patch on halftone
  scans.

So Remove Selection tries the hidden version first, checks if the result
came back flat, and if so reruns with the original visible. Fills go the
other way around, because new content should land on the real texture.

Generative Fill had one more problem: the new object came out cut by the
selection border. Seeing the real background, the model has no idea where
the area ends. The fix was cropping tight around the selection, because
the models do frame an object well inside the picture they get, and adding
"shown whole and centered" to the prompt. After that, 6 out of 6 seeds
came out whole. **FLUX.2 klein** does add, replace and erase-text in one
pass of 12 to 35 seconds. **Qwen** adds instead of replacing (it kept the
backpack and put a small cat next to it), so with **Qwen** I remove first,
then fill.

On the way I also found a bug in the upstream GIMP AI plug-in: in Focused
mode it built an empty mask unless the selection started at the top left
corner. Online models hid it. A strict local model just returned the image
unchanged. Patched in my copy.

Not everything moved to local. Restyling a drawing into another artist's
style failed with both models, **Qwen** gave generic anime and drifted the
character, so that stays on paid tools.

## Where it ended up

Mini AI is still on GitHub and still works for what the tests say it works
for: quick questions, searches, translations, reading a PDF, checking an
inbox. Goose with the 30B model is fine for file and search tasks, if I
give it time.

But for me the real value of free models turned out to be small jobs inside
a pipeline, where a script does the thinking and the model does one thing.
Describe a page, fill a hole in an image, erase the text from a comic, fix
a scratch on an old photo, give a Digimon a personality. For that they're
great, and they cost nothing.

For the open-ended work, planning, writing code across a repo, recovering
from its own mistakes, I still use **Claude**. Small models are too dumb to
trust, and big models are too slow on a 6 GB GPU. Maybe the next laptop
changes that.
